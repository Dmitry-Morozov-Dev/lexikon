Why collocations matter
Collocations, words that commonly occur together, are a natural and essential part of
English. Acquiring collocations is a key step in improving overall English proficiency.



About the Academic Collocation List
Created by Pearson, the Academic Collocation List contains the most frequent and
pedagogically relevant lexical collocations in written academic English, helping learners
recognize and use words that naturally co-occur rather than studying words in isolation.

   A Number of collocations: 2,469

   A Source: Pearson International Corpus of Academic English (25 mill words)

   A Purpose: to support PTE preparation


Benefits and use
In highlighting the most important cross-disciplinary collocations, the Academic
Collocation List can help learners increase their collocational competence and thus
their proficiency in academic English.

The Academic Collocation List can also support PTE teachers in their lesson planning
and serve as a tool for improving academic language development in their learners.

How to use the Academic Collocation List
The Academic Collocation List can be used in the classroom to help learners notice,
record, and use collocations effectively across PTE questions:

Understand collocations

   A Introduce learners to common word combinations (e.g., verb + noun, adjective
     + noun, and noun + noun) to improve natural language use.

       Example: Provide examples of collocations from the Academic Collocation List.


 verb + noun                   adjective + noun                       noun + noun

 have access (to)              hierarchical structure                 information flow

 have consequences                                                    information gathering

 have limitations                                                     information processing


Record and learn

   A Encourage learners to notice collocations in sample prompts (texts or audio),
     record them in notebooks or digital logs, and review them regularly.

       Example: Learners notice the collocation prime example. They then go to
       the Academic Collocation List to find other collocations with prime, such as
       prime time.

Extend vocabulary

   A Use collocations as a springboard to related words and phrases, broadening
     learners’ academic vocabulary.

       Example: Starting with a collocation from the Academic Collocation List, such as
       conduct research, learners can explore related academic expressions like carry
       out a study, investigate, or analyze data.

Use in PTE Speaking & Writing questions

  A Encourage learners to actively include collocations in responses to PTE
    Speaking & Writing questions, improving range and precision.

     Example: For a Describe Image question showing a rising bar chart of population
     growth:

     1. The teacher selects collocations from the Academic Collocation List, for
        example, significant growth and reach (a) peak.
     2. Learners are asked to incorporate these collocations into their descriptions of
        the image.
     Sample learner response: “The chart shows that the population has experienced
     significant growth over the last decade, reaching a peak in 2020.”

Use in PTE Reading & Listening questions

  A Encourage learners to recognize and apply collocations accurately to improve
    comprehension and maximize their scores.

     Example: For Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown) or Fill in the Blanks (Drag and Drop)
     question, learners should use the Academic Collocation List to identify the most
     appropriate collocation for each gap. They can also use the Academic
     Collocation List to find alternative words that naturally collocate with the missing
     word or one of the answer options, ensuring that all choices sound authentic and
     contextually appropriate.

Reinforce through practice

  A Incorporate collocations into classroom activities such as gap-fills, matching
    exercises, and quizzes.

     Example:

     1. The teacher selects collocations from the Academic Collocation List to design
        a matching exercise.

                verb                            noun

                take                            aware

                make                            responsibility

                become                          arrangements


     2. Learners complete the matching exercise (e.g., take responsibility, make
        arrangements, become aware), then refer to the Academic Collocation List to
        explore additional collocations for each verb.
